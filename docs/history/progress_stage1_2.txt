2. Models implemented

Expanding, rolling-20, rolling-60, EWMA (λ=0.94, configurable), GARCH(1,1), EGARCH(1,1), GJR-GARCH(1,1), each with Gaussian and Student-t innovations. Zero mean, ×100 scaling converted back to decimal variance, 1000-session rolling window (min 500), refit every 21 sessions with O(1) state updates verified against arch's own filter. Markov-switching, XGBoost, latent SV, HAR-RV, Realized GARCH are registered as pending; Heston is marked NOT IMPLEMENTED (no options data exists, confirmed by data-check).

6. Main observations

- Asymmetric GARCH beats GARCH(1,1) by 0.03–0.05 QLIKE on every asset, same sign in both periods, but only SPY EGARCH in validation survives Holm correction.
- Student-t innovations add nothing to one-step variance forecasts (|ΔQLIKE| ≤ 0.003) and cause the only instabilities (VT EGARCH-t).
- Historical benchmarks are significantly worse than GARCH(1,1) in several cases but are the best calibrated; GARCH-family models over-forecast 30–45% in post-shock recoveries.
- GJR adapts fastest to shocks; the expanding variance fails everywhere. Patterns are consistent across the three markets.

7. Unresolved issues

EGARCH-t fragility on VT (degenerate fits with ν at the 2.05 bound), GJR alpha at zero in 50–70% of refits, few shock observations for regime tables, no intraday or options data for Stage 4, partial 2026 sample.

8. Stage 3 recommendations

Advance gjr_normal, egarch_normal, garch_normal, and ewma_094; drop Student-t variants. Implement Markov-switching and XGBoost against these, tune on development, select on validation, keep the holdout sealed, and add a model confidence set.
